# Formula C: "How You Invented X" (the evolution ladder)

Measured 2026-11-12 from two shorts that each passed ~14M views in 13 days:
- *How a Hole on a Beach Becomes a Bunker* (62 s, 7 rungs, ~200 words, ~3.2 words/s, 15.3M views)
- *How you invented the door* (34 s, 5 rungs, ~95 words, ~2.8 words/s, 13.9M views, 4.7% engagement)
Study only. Never copy their lines, objects, or shots. Topic, script and look are always OUR OWN.

## The idea in one line
The viewer ("you") starts with the most primitive version of a thing. One enemy or one force keeps beating it, every
fix fails in a way you can SEE, and step by step the thing becomes the object we all know. The last line names that object.

## Structure
| # | Beat | Time | Template |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook: primitive version + the threat** | 0-3 s | "If you [primitive act] to [goal/threat], you have a [primitive name]." or "You're [relatable, slightly embarrassing situation], but [antagonist] keeps [problem]." |
| 2 | **Rungs** (×4-7, each 5-8 s) | 3-50 s | "But [the force/enemy beats it physically]. So you [verb + material + where]. This is a [new name]." |
| 3 | **Twist rung** (optional, strong) | ~last 15 s | "That [feature] isn't [odd] by mistake. [The hidden reason in one line]." Answers "why does it look like that?" |
| 4 | **Cosy tag** | ~5 s | A list of three: two normal items, the third absurd or funny (for us: a researched real fact). |
| 5 | **Payoff** | last 2 s | "Congratulations, you have built a [object]." or a callback to the antagonist. Matches the title, so it loops. |

Title = start state → end state: "How a [primitive] Becomes a [object]" / "How you invented the [object]".

## Rules
- **Best jokes = real history inside the ladder.** Pick a topic where each rung really happened (a real tower, a real
  failure), then use its strangest true detail as the joke line (Eddystone: the builder who wished for the greatest storm).
- **Second person, present tense.** No names, places or dates in the hook. A dated real fact may appear mid-ladder as an aside (a joke).
- **Connectors alternate So / But / Except. Never "and then".** Every rung is a but/therefore.
- **Every failure is physical cause and effect** (wave, wind, fire, ladder, tide), drawable in one shot. No abstract failures ("it was too expensive").
- **Every fix is mechanical and concrete:** a verb, a material and a placement ("weld three beams into an X").
- **A false relief now and then** ("Clever." "Much better.") right before the next failure. The viewer learns to predict the failure, and that keeps them watching.
- **Name each stage** ("This is a keep.") as a small reward. Name about every second rung, not all of them.
- **One antagonist for the whole ladder** (the raiders, the nosy friend). It turns a list into a story.
- **Escalation:** every attack is cleverer than the last. The final fix is the least obvious one.
- Length: 160-200 words for 55-65 s, or 90-110 words for a 30-35 s version. Sentences ≤ 14 words.
- The house rules still win: English, no captions in the video, VO with no pauses, jokes are researched real facts, recorded SFX only.

## Picture and camera (measured)
- **ONE set that grows.** The object changes every 5-8 s, so retention comes from the transformation, not from cuts.
  This is cheaper than formula B: one set, built in stages (`stage` props toggled on word times).
- **Each rung = failure shot + build shot.** Failure: the force hits and the object breaks, floods or opens (shake + SFX).
  Build: the new part assembles itself or is hammered on, timed to the noun ("hinges", "steel beams").
- **Cutaway cross-sections** for anything underground or inside (a tunnel, a hole, a wall's thickness).
- Camera keeps moving: whip-pans and continuous pushes between rungs, hard cuts only on impacts.
- The stage name ("This is a keep") gets a clean hero angle of the whole object for about 1 s.
- **Ending: a big pull-back** to the finished object with every layer working at once (all the past rungs visible).
- The diorama style (high camera, toy model) fits this formula naturally: the toy model "grows".
